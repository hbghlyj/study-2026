# 1.
Give an example of a complete, noncompact Riemannian manifold where every point lies on a closed geodesic.

**Proof.**

A flat two-dimensional cylinder, e.g., $S^1 \times \mathbb{R}$ with the product metric, is a complete, noncompact Riemannian manifold. For any point $p=(x,y) \in S^1 \times \mathbb{R}$, the circle $S^1 \times \{y\}$ is a closed geodesic containing $p$.

# 2.
Prove that any locally minimizing admissible path is a critical point of $L(\cdot)$.

**Proof**

Then for every $c \in [a,b]$ there exists $\delta>0$
such that for every admissible variation
$\gamma_s$ with $|s|<\delta$ we have

$$
L(\gamma_s) \ge L(\gamma).
$$

Hence the derivative at $s=0$ must be zero:

$$
\left.\frac{d}{ds}L(\gamma_s)\right|_{s=0}=0.
$$

Therefore $\gamma$ is a critical point of the length functional.

By the last theorem,
every critical point is a geodesic.
Let $\Gamma$ be a variation and let

$$
V = \frac{\partial \Gamma}{\partial s}.
$$

Without loss of generality we may assume $s \in I$ is small,
otherwise we replace $\Gamma$ by $\exp(sV)$.

Then
$$
L_g(\Gamma_s)
$$

is a smooth function of $s$, and since $\gamma$ is a local minimizer,

$$
L_g(\Gamma_0) \le L_g(\Gamma_s)
\quad \text{for all } s \in I.
$$

By Rolle's theorem,

$$
\left.\frac{d}{ds} L_g(\Gamma_s)\right|_{s=0} = 0.
$$

Since the $\Gamma$ was arbitrary, $\gamma$ is a critical point of $L_g$,
and therefore $\gamma$ is a geodesic.
