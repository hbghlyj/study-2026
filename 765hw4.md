# 2.
Show that a flat connection over a simply-connected manifold is globally isomorphic (gauge-equivalent) to the product connection.

**Proof**

To construct the global trivialization of a rank $r$-bundle $P \to M$ equipped with a flat connection $\nabla$, where $M$ is connected and simply connected, proceed as follows.

Fix a base point $x_0 \in M$ and choose a point $p_0 \in P_{x_0}$ in the fiber over $x_0$. Flatness of the connection implies that parallel transport along paths in $M$ is path-independent, because any two paths with the same endpoints are homotopic and the curvature vanishes.

Define a global section $s: M \to P$ by declaring $s(x)$ to be the value obtained by parallel transporting $p_0$ along any path from $x_0$ to $x$. This is well-defined due to path independence.

The resulting section $s$ is parallel (covariantly constant), because parallel transport along any path from a point $x$ to a point $y$ maps $s(x)$ to $s(y)$: the concatenated path from $x_0$ to $y$ via $x$ yields $s(y)$, and path independence ensures consistency. Consequently, the tangent vectors to the image of $s$ are horizontal with respect to $\nabla$, and the pull-back connection $s^*\nabla$ vanishes identically.

This yields a global trivialization $P \cong M \times \mathbb{R}^r$, given explicitly by the map $P \to M \times \mathbb{R}^r$ sending $p \mapsto (\pi(p), g)$ where $p = s(\pi(p)) \cdot g$ using the right $GL$-action. In this trivialization, the connection corresponds to the product connection on $M \times \mathbb{R}^r$, which is the standard trivial (flat) connection with zero connection form.


# 3.
Recall that $\pi_1\left(S^1\right) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Define a 1-dimensional real representation, i.e. an action of $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ on $x \in \mathbb{R}$, by

$$x \mapsto(-1)^n \cdot x .$$

Show that the vector bundle associated to this representation (under the correspondence stated during class) is the Möbius bundle.

**Proof**

The vector bundle is constructed as the quotient of the trivial bundle over the universal cover of $S^1$ by an action of the fundamental group $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. The universal cover of $S^1$ is $\mathbb{R}$, so we consider the trivial bundle $\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$.

The action of $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ on a point $(t, x) \in \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$ (where $t$ is in the universal cover and $x$ is in the fiber) is given by:
$$n \cdot (t, x) = (t+n, (-1)^n x)$$
The associated bundle is the quotient space $E = (\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}) / \mathbb{Z}$. This is the Möbius bundle because as we traverse a loop in the base $S^1$ (e.g., from $t=0$ to $t=1$, corresponding to $n=1$), the fiber is inverted ($x \mapsto -x$), which is the defining twist of the Möbius strip.

# 6.
Let $A$ be a connection on $E$ with local curvature components $F_{ij}{}^\alpha{}_\beta$. Show that the curvature of the induced connection on $E^*$ works as follows:

$$\left(F\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}, \frac{\partial}{\partial x^j}\right) t\right)_\beta=-t_\alpha F_{ij}{}^\alpha{}_\beta$$

**Proof**
In a local coordinate neighborhood on the manifold $M$, choose a local frame $\{e_a\}$ for the vector bundle $V$ (with dual frame $\{\theta^a\}$ for the dual bundle $V^*$). The original connection $\nabla$ acts on the frame as
$\nabla_{\partial/\partial x^j} e_b = \Gamma^a_{b j} \, e_a,$
where $\Gamma^a_{b j}$ are the connection coefficients (generalized Christoffel symbols) in this frame.
The dual connection $\nabla^*$ on $V^*$ is then given by its action on the dual frame:
$\nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^b = -\Gamma^a_{b j} \, \theta^a.$
This expression arises directly from applying the defining product rule to the constant pairing $\theta^a(e_b) = \delta^a_b$, yielding the negative sign and the transposition of the lower indices on the connection coefficients.
The defining feature of the dual connection $\nabla^*$ is that it preserves the natural pairing between a vector bundle and its dual. If you take a local frame of vectors $e_b$ and its dual frame of covectors $\theta^a$, their pairing is a constant (the Kronecker delta):
$\langle \theta^a, e_b \rangle = \delta^a_b$
Because this pairing is a constant (either 1 when $a = b$, or 0 otherwise), its directional derivative is simply zero. When we apply the Leibniz product rule to this pairing along a coordinate direction $\partial/\partial x^j$, we get:
$0 = \langle \nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^a, e_b \rangle + \langle \theta^a, \nabla_{\partial/\partial x^j} e_b \rangle$
We already know how the original connection acts on the vector frame: $\nabla_{\partial/\partial x^j} e_b = \Gamma^c_{b j} e_c$. Substituting this in gives:
$0 = \langle \nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^a, e_b \rangle + \langle \theta^a, \Gamma^c_{b j} e_c \rangle$
$0 = \langle \nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^a, e_b \rangle + \Gamma^c_{b j} \delta^a_c$
$0 = \langle \nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^a, e_b \rangle + \Gamma^a_{b j}$
Moving the connection term to the other side of the equation yields the negative sign:
$\langle \nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^a, e_b \rangle = -\Gamma^a_{b j}$
For a general section $\phi = \phi_b \, \theta^b$ of $V^*$, the coordinate expression for the dual connection is
$\nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \phi = \left( \partial_j \phi_b - \Gamma^a_{b j} \phi_a \right) \theta^b,$
where summation over repeated indices is understood. This follows by applying $\nabla^*$ using the Leibniz rule and substituting the action on the dual frame.[30]
In matrix form, if the connection matrices for $\nabla$ (with entries $\Gamma^a_{b j}$) are denoted $\Gamma_j$, the corresponding matrices for $\nabla^*$ are $-\Gamma_j^T$, reflecting the transposition and sign change characteristic of the induced dual connection.
