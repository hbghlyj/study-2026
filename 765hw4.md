# 1.
Let $U \cong B_1(0)$ be a coordinate ball centered at $x_0 \in M$ and $V \cong B_1(0)$ a coordinate ball centered at $y_0 \in M$. Suppose that $U \cap V$ is nonempty and connected.

Let $A$ be a flat connection on $E \rightarrow M$. Show that the transition function between the adapted frame at $x_0 \in U$ and the adapted frame at $y_0 \in V$ is constant.

**Proof**

In an adapted frame $e = (e_1, \dots, e_m)$ on the coordinate ball $U \cong B_1(0)$ centered at $x_0 = 0$, the connection $\nabla$ on the rank-$m$ vector bundle is represented by a matrix-valued 1-form $`A = \sum_{i=1}^n A_i \  dx^i`$, where each $A_i(x)$ is an $m \times m$ matrix-valued function and $\nabla_{\partial_i} e = e A_i$.

The adapted conditions consist of two parts. 
1. the radial gauge condition $\nabla_{\vec{r}} e \equiv 0$, where $\vec{r} = \sum_{i=1}^n x^i \partial / \partial x^i$ is the radial vector field, yields

    $\sum_{i=1}^n x^i A_i(x) = 0 \quad \text{for all } x \in U.$

2. the vanishing condition at the center $\nabla e (0) = 0$ implies $A_i(0) = 0$ for each $i = 1, \dots, n$.

The flatness of the connection implies that the curvature 2-form vanishes, so $F = dA + A \wedge A = 0$. In components, this means $F_{ij} = \partial_i A_j - \partial_j A_i + [A_i, A_j] = 0$ for all $i,j$.

Differentiating the adapted condition $\sum_{j=1}^n x^j A_j(x) = 0$ with respect to $\partial_i$ yields $A_i + \sum_{j=1}^n x^j \partial_i A_j = 0$, or equivalently $\sum_{j=1}^n x^j \partial_i A_j = -A_i$.

Contract the flatness condition with $x^j$ by summing $x^j F_{ji} = 0$ over $j$:

$0 = \sum_j x^j (\partial_j A_i - \partial_i A_j + [A_j, A_i]) = \sum_j x^j \partial_j A_i - \sum_j x^j \partial_i A_j + \sum_j x^j [A_j, A_i].$

The commutator term simplifies to $[\sum_j x^j A_j, A_i] = [0, A_i] = 0$. The middle term is $-\sum_j x^j \partial_i A_j = -(-A_i) = A_i$. The first term is the radial derivative $\vec{r}(A_i)$. Thus,

$\vec{r}(A_i) + A_i = 0.$

Fix a point $p \in U$ and consider the ray $x(t) = t p$ for $t \in [0,1]$, so $x(0) = 0$ and $x(1) = p$. Along this ray, $\vec{r} = t \frac{d}{dt}$, and the equation becomes

$t \frac{d}{dt} A_i(tp) + A_i(tp) = 0,$

or equivalently

$\frac{d}{dt} \bigl( t A_i(tp) \bigr) = 0.$

Hence $t A_i(tp)$ is constant in $t$. Since $A_i(0) = 0$, taking the limit as $t \to 0$ shows the constant is 0. Therefore $t A_i(tp) = 0$ for all $t \in (0,1]$, implying $A_i(tp) = 0$.

As $p \in U$ is arbitrary, $A_i(x) = 0$ for all $x \in U$ and all $i$. Thus the connection form $A$ vanishes identically on $U$ in the adapted frame $e$.

The identical argument applied on $V$ shows that the connection form in the adapted frame $f$ vanishes identically on $V$.

Since the adapted frames on $U$ and $V$ are both parallel on the intersection $U \cap V$ (with vanishing connection forms $A^{(e)} \equiv 0$ and $A^{(f)} \equiv 0$), the transition function $g : U \cap V \to \mathrm{GL}(m)$ defined by $f = e g$ satisfies the gauge transformation law for the connection form:

$A^{(f)} = g^{-1} dg + g^{-1} A^{(e)} g$.

Substituting the vanishing forms gives $0 = g^{-1} dg$, or equivalently $dg = 0$ on $U \cap V$.

Thus $g$ is locally constant on $U \cap V$. Because $U \cap V$ is connected by assumption, $g$ must be constant on the entire intersection.

This shows that the transition function between the two adapted frames is a constant element of $\mathrm{GL}(m)$.

# 2.
Show that a flat connection over a simply-connected manifold is globally isomorphic (gauge-equivalent) to the product connection.

**Proof**

To construct the global trivialization of a rank $r$ vector bundle $E \to M$ equipped with a flat connection $\nabla$, where $M$ is connected and simply connected, proceed as follows.

Fix a base point $x_0 \in M$ and choose a basis $`\{e_{0,1}, \dots, e_{0,r}\}`$ for the fiber $E_{x_0}$. Since the connection is flat and $M$ is simply connected, parallel transport is path-independent. For each $i=1, \dots, r$, define a global section $s_i: M \to E$ by parallel transporting $e_{0,i}$ from $x_0$ to any point $x \in M$.

The sections $`\{s_1, \dots, s_r\}`$ form a global frame of parallel sections (i.e., $\nabla s_i = 0$ for all $i$). This frame defines a global trivialization $\Phi: E \to M \times \mathbb{R}^r$. For any vector $p \in E_x$, we can uniquely write it as a linear combination $p = \sum_{i=1}^r c^i s_i(x)$. The map is given by $\Phi(p) = (\pi(p), (c^1, \dots, c^r))$. This shows $E \cong M \times \mathbb{R}^r$, and in this trivialization, the connection is the product  connection.

# 3.
Recall that $\pi_1\left(S^1\right) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Define a 1-dimensional real representation, i.e. an action of $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ on $x \in \mathbb{R}$, by

$$x \mapsto(-1)^n \cdot x .$$

Show that the vector bundle associated to this representation (under the correspondence stated during class) is the Möbius bundle.

**Proof**

The vector bundle is constructed as the quotient of the trivial bundle over the universal cover of $S^1$ by an action of the fundamental group $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. The universal cover of $S^1$ is $\mathbb{R}$, so we consider the trivial bundle $\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$.

The action of $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ on a point $(t, x) \in \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$ (where $t$ is in the universal cover and $x$ is in the fiber) is given by:
$$n \cdot (t, x) = (t+n, (-1)^n x)$$
The associated bundle is the quotient space $E = (\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}) / \mathbb{Z}$. This is the Möbius bundle because as we traverse a loop in the base $S^1\cong \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ (e.g., from $t=0$ to $t=1$, corresponding to $n=1$), the fiber is inverted ($x \mapsto -x$), which is the defining twist of the Möbius strip.

Consider the tautological complex line bundle $\mathscr{O}(-1) \rightarrow \mathbb{C P}^1$. Let $`U_0=\{[z, 1] \mid z \in \mathbb{C}\}`$ and $`U_1=\{[1, w] \mid w \in \mathbb{C}\}`$. Choose frames

$$e^{(0)}([z, 1])=(z, 1)$$

over $U_0$ and

$$e^{(1)}([1, w])=(1, w) .$$

over $U_1$. Define connection forms

$$A^{(0)}=\frac{\bar{z} dz}{1+|z|^2}$$

on $U_0$ and

$$A^{(1)}=\frac{\bar{w} dw}{1+|w|^2}$$

(a) Show that these give a well-defined connection $A$ on $\mathscr{O}(-1) \rightarrow \mathbb{C P}^1$.

**Proof**

On $U_0\cap U_1$, $e^{(0)}([z:1])=(z,1)=z\ (1,1/z)=z\ e^{(1)}([1:w])$, so $e^{(1)} = g_{01}\  e^{(0)}$ with $g_{01}=\frac{1}{z}$.

The connection 1-forms satisfy $A^{(1)}=A^{(0)}+g_{01}^{-1}dg_{01}$ to ensure the connection is well-defined.

Here, $g_{01}^{-1}dg_{01}=z\ d\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)= -\frac{dz}{z}$.

Rewriting $A^{(1)}$ in the $z$-coordinate yields

$A^{(1)}=\frac{\overline{(1/z)} (-1/z^2) dz}{1+|1/z|^2}= -\frac{dz}{z(1+|z|^2)}$

$A^{(0)} + g_{01}^{-1}dg_{01} = \frac{\bar z\ dz}{1+|z|^2} - \frac{dz}{z} = -\frac{dz}{z(1+|z|^2)}$, confirming consistency. Thus, the 1-forms define a global connection.

(b) Compute its curvature.

**Proof**

$F=dA+A\wedge A$. For a line bundle, $A$ is a $u(1)$-valued 1-form, so $A\wedge A = 0$. Thus, the curvature is $F = dA$ locally.

On $U_0$, $dA^{(0)} = \frac{1}{(1+|z|^2)^2}\  d\bar z\wedge dz$.

On $U_1$, $dA^{(1)} = \frac{1}{(1+|w|^2)^2}\  d\bar w\wedge dw$.

These expressions are consistent under the coordinate change $w=1/z$.

(c) Use your answer from (b) to calculate the first Chern class of $\mathscr{O}(-1)$.

**Proof**

The de Rham representative of the first Chern class is $c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = \left[\frac{i}{2\pi}F\right]\in H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{CP}^1)$.

Use $z=re^{i\theta}$, so $d\bar z\wedge dz = 2i\  r\ dr\wedge d\theta$. Then $F = \frac{2i\  r}{(1+r^2)^2}\ dr\wedge d\theta$, and

$\frac{i}{2\pi}F = -\frac{r}{\pi(1+r^2)^2}\ dr\wedge d\theta$.

Integrating over $\mathbb{CP}^1$ ( $r\in[0,\infty)$, $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$ ) gives

$\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\frac{i}{2\pi}F = -\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{r}{(1+r^2)^2}\ dr\ d\theta = -\frac{2\pi}{\pi} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = -1,$

since $\int_0^\infty \frac{r}{(1+r^2)^2}\ dr = [-\frac1{2(1+r^2)}]_0^\infty=\frac{1}{2}$. Thus, $c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -1\in \mathbb{Z} \cong H^2(\mathbb{CP}^1;\mathbb{Z})$.

# 6.
Let $A$ be a connection on $E$ with local curvature components $F_{ij}{}^\alpha{}_\beta$. Show that the curvature of the induced connection on $E^*$ works as follows:

$$\left(F\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}, \frac{\partial}{\partial x^j}\right) t\right)_\beta=-t_\alpha F_{ij}{}^\alpha{}_\beta$$

**Proof**

In a local coordinate neighborhood on the manifold $M$, choose a local frame $`\{e_a\}`$ for the vector bundle $V$ (with dual frame $`\{\theta^a\}`$ for the dual bundle $V^*$). The original connection $\nabla$ acts on the frame as

$\nabla_{\partial/\partial x^j} e_b = A^a_{j b} e_a,$

where $A^a_{j b}$ are the connection matrices in this frame.

The dual connection $`\nabla^*`$ on $V^*$ is then given by its action on the dual frame:

$`\nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^b = -A^a_{j b} \theta^a.`$

This expression arises directly from applying the defining product rule to the constant pairing $\theta^a(e_b) = \delta^a_b$, yielding the negative sign and the transposition of the lower indices on the connection coefficients.

The defining feature of the dual connection $\nabla^*$ is that it preserves the natural pairing between a vector bundle and its dual. If you take a local frame of vectors $e_b$ and its dual frame of covectors $\theta^a$, their pairing is a constant (the Kronecker delta):

$\langle \theta^a, e_b \rangle = \delta^a_b$

Because this pairing is a constant (either 1 when $a = b$, or 0 otherwise), its directional derivative is simply zero. When we apply the Leibniz product rule to this pairing along a coordinate direction $\partial/\partial x^j$, we get:

$`0 = \langle \nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^a, e_b \rangle + \langle \theta^a, \nabla_{\partial/\partial x^j} e_b \rangle`$

We already know how the original connection acts on the vector frame: $\nabla_{\partial/\partial x^j} e_b = A^c_{j b} e_c$. Substituting this in gives:

$`0 = \langle \nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^a, e_b \rangle + \langle \theta^a, A^c_{j b} e_c \rangle`$

$`0 = \langle \nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^a, e_b \rangle + A^c_{j b} \delta^a_c`$

$`0 = \langle \nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^a, e_b \rangle + A^a_{j b}`$

Moving the connection term to the other side of the equation yields the negative sign:

$`\langle \nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \theta^a, e_b \rangle = -A^a_{j b}`$

For a general section $`\phi = \phi_b \  \theta^b`$ of $V^*$, the coordinate expression for the dual connection is

$`\nabla^*_{\partial/\partial x^j} \phi = \left( \partial_j \phi_b - A^a_{j b} \phi_a \right) \theta^b,`$

where summation over repeated indices is understood. This follows by applying $\nabla^*$ using the Leibniz rule and substituting the action on the dual frame.

In matrix form, if the connection matrices for $\nabla$ (with entries $A^a_{j b}$) are denoted $A_j$, the corresponding matrices for $\nabla^*$ are $-A_j^T$, reflecting the transposition and sign change characteristic of the induced dual connection.
