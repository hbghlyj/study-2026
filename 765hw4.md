# 1.
Let $U \cong B_1(0)$ be a coordinate ball centered at $x_0 \in M$ and $V \cong B_1(0)$ a coordinate ball centered at $y_0 \in M$. Suppose that $U \cap V$ is a nonempty and connected.

Let $A$ be a flat connection on $E \rightarrow M$. Show that the transition function between the adapted frame at $x_0 \in U$ and the adapted frame at $y_0 \in V$ is constant.

# 3.
Recall that $\pi_1\left(S^1\right) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Define a 1-dimensional real representation, i.e. an action of $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ on $x \in \mathbb{R}$, by

$$x \mapsto(-1)^n \cdot x .$$

Show that the vector bundle associated to this representation (under the correspondence stated during class) is the Möbius bundle.

we can quotient by $\pi_1(M)$ and obtain the Möbius bundle with a flat connection from the product connection.

# 4.
Consider the tautological complex line bundle $\mathscr{O}(-1) \rightarrow \mathbb{C P}^1$. Let $U_0=\{[z, 1] \mid z \in \mathbb{C}\}$ and $U_1=\{[1, w] \mid w \in \mathbb{C}\}$. Choose frames

$$e^{(0)}([z, 1])=(z, 1)$$

over $U_0$ and

$$e^{(1)}([1, w])=(1, w) .$$

over $U_1$. Define connection forms

$$A^{(0)}=\frac{\bar{z} dz}{1+|z|^2}$$

on $U_0$ and

$$A^{(1)}=\frac{\bar{w} dw}{1+|w|^2}$$

(a) Show that these give a well-defined connection $A$ on $\mathscr{O}(-1) \rightarrow \mathbb{C P}^1$.

(b) Compute its curvature.

(c) Use your answer from (b) to calculate the first Chern class of $\mathscr{O}(-1)$.

# 5.
Recall from class that if $A$ and $B$ are connections on $E$ and $F$, respectively, then the induced connection on $E \otimes F$ should take the following local form. Letting $\left\{e_\alpha\right\}$ and $\left\{f_\beta\right\}$ be local frames for $E$ and $F$, for a local section $\omega=\omega^{\alpha \beta} e_\alpha \otimes f_\beta$, we have

$$\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}} \omega=\left(\frac{\partial \omega^{\alpha \beta}}{\partial x^i}+A_{i \gamma}^\alpha \omega^{\gamma \beta}+B_{i \gamma}^\beta \omega^{\alpha \gamma}\right) e_\alpha \otimes f_\beta .$$

Check that the connection so defined indeed satisfies the Leibniz rule:

$$\nabla_X(s \otimes t)=\nabla_X s \otimes t+s \otimes \nabla_X t .$$
