# 1.
Are the spaces $S^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^4$ and $S^4 \times \mathbb{RP}^2$ homotopy equivalent? Justify your answer!

using Z/2 cohomology and the Künneth theorem.

Proof

The cohomology rings with $\mathbb{Z}_2$-coefficients are $H^*(S^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^4 ; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2[x]/(x^2) \times \mathbb{Z}_2[y]/(y^5)$ and $H^*(S^4 \times \mathbb{RP}^2 ; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2[a]/(a^2) \times \mathbb{Z}_2[b]/(b^3)$, where $x$ and $a$ are the generators of the cohomology of the spheres, and $y$ and $b$ are the generators of the cohomology of the projective spaces. The ring structures differ: the first has a generator in degree 2 and a generator in degree 4, while the second has a generator in degree 4 and a generator in degree 2. To be more precise, the first ring has a nontrivial product $x \cup y$ in degree 6, while the second ring has a nontrivial product $a \cup b$ in degree 6. However, the first ring has a nontrivial product $x \cup x$ in degree 4, while the second ring has a trivial product $a \cup a$ in degree 8. This difference in the ring structure implies that the two spaces cannot be homotopy equivalent, as homotopy equivalences induce isomorphisms of cohomology rings.
