# 1.
Are the spaces $S^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^4$ and $S^4 \times \mathbb{RP}^2$ homotopy equivalent? Justify your answer!

Proof

The cohomology rings with $\mathbb{Z}_2$-coefficients are:

$H^*(S^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^4 ; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2[x]/(x^2) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_2[y]/(y^5)$, where $x$ is in degree 2 and $y$ is in degree 1.

$H^*(S^4 \times \mathbb{RP}^2 ; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2[a]/(a^2) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_2[b]/(b^3)$, where $a$ is in degree 4 and $b$ is in degree 1.

We compare the dimensions of the cohomology groups in each degree.

For $H^*(S^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^4 ; \mathbb{Z}_2)$:

$H^0 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$

$H^1 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$ (generated by $y$)

$H^2 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}_2$ (generated by $x$ and $y^2$).

For $H^*(S^4 \times \mathbb{RP}^2 ; \mathbb{Z}_2)$:

$H^0 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$

$H^1 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$ (generated by $b$)

$H^2 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$ (generated by $b^2$).

Since the dimension of $H^2(S^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^4 ; \mathbb{Z}_2)$ is 2 and the dimension of $H^2(S^4 \times \mathbb{RP}^2 ; \mathbb{Z}_2)$ is 1, the cohomology rings are not isomorphic. Therefore, the spaces $S^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^4$ and $S^4 \times \mathbb{RP}^2$ are not homotopy equivalent.

# 2.
Using cup products, show that every map $S^{k+l} \rightarrow S^k \times S^l$ induces the trivial homomorphism $H_{k+l}(S^{k+l}) \rightarrow H_{k+l}(S^k \times S^l)$, assuming $k>0$ and $l>0$.

**Proof**

Let $f: S^{k+l} \to S^k \times S^l$ be any continuous map. The induced map on cohomology $`f^*: H^*(S^k \times S^l; \mathbb{Z}) \to H^*(S^{k+l}; \mathbb{Z})`$ is a ring homomorphism. Let $\alpha \in H^k(S^k \times S^l; \mathbb{Z})$ and $\beta \in H^l(S^k \times S^l; \mathbb{Z})$ be the pullbacks of the generators of $H^k(S^k; \mathbb{Z})$ and $H^l(S^l; \mathbb{Z})$ via the projections. Since $k,l > 0$, the groups $H^k(S^{k+l}; \mathbb{Z})$ and $H^l(S^{k+l}; \mathbb{Z})$ are trivial. As $`f^*\alpha \in H^k(S^{k+l}; \mathbb{Z})`$ and $`f^*\beta \in H^l(S^{k+l}; \mathbb{Z})`$, we must have $`f^*\alpha = 0`$ and $`f^*\beta = 0`$.

By naturality of the cup product, $`f^*(\alpha \smile \beta) = f^*\alpha \smile f^*\beta = 0 \smile 0 = 0`$. The generator of $H^{k+l}(S^k \times S^l; \mathbb{Z})$ is precisely $\alpha \smile \beta$ (up to units and signs), so $`f^*`$ vanishes on the top-dimensional cohomology class. Since both $H_{k+l}(S^{k+l}; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $H_{k+l}(S^k \times S^l; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ are free of rank one, and the induced map $`f_*: H_{k+l}(S^{k+l}; \mathbb{Z}) \to H_{k+l}(S^k \times S^l; \mathbb{Z})`$ is dual to $`f^*`$ on cohomology (via the pairing of homology and cohomology classes), the vanishing of $f^*$ on the generator of $H^{k+l}(S^k \times S^l; \mathbb{Z})$ implies that $`f_*`$ vanishes on the generator of $H_{k+l}(S^{k+l}; \mathbb{Z})$. Thus $`f_*`$ is the zero homomorphism.

# 3.
Describe $`H^*(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty} / \mathbb{CP}^1 ; \mathbb{Z})`$ as a ring with finitely many multiplicative generators. How does this ring compare with $`H^*(S^6 \times \mathbb{HP}^{\infty} ; \mathbb{Z})`$?

**Proof**

The cohomology ring of the quotient $\mathbb{CP}^{\infty} / \mathbb{CP}^1$ can be computed using the long exact sequence in cohomology associated to the pair $(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}, \mathbb{CP}^1)$ and the isomorphism $`H^*(X,A) \cong \tilde{H}^*(X/A)`$ for a good pair $(X,A)$. This implies that $`\tilde{H}^*(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}/\mathbb{CP}^1; \mathbb{Z})`$ is isomorphic as a ring to the kernel of the map $`i^*: H^*(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}; \mathbb{Z}) \to H^*(\mathbb{CP}^1; \mathbb{Z})`$ in positive degrees.

Given $`H^*(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]`$ and $`H^*(\mathbb{CP}^1; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}[y]/(y^2)`$ (with $\deg x = \deg y = 2$), and the inclusion $\mathbb{CP}^1 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{CP}^{\infty}$ induces a ring homomorphism $i^*$ sending $x$ to $y$. The kernel of $`i^*`$ is the ideal $(x^2) \subset \mathbb{Z}[x]$. This ring is generated as a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra by $x^2$ (in degree 4) and $x^3$ (in degree 6).

For comparison, by the Künneth formula, $`H^*(S^6 \times \mathbb{HP}^{\infty} ; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H^*(S^6; \mathbb{Z}) \otimes H^*(\mathbb{HP}^{\infty} ; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}[s]/(s^2) \otimes \mathbb{Z}[w] \cong \mathbb{Z}[s,w]/(s^2)`$, where $\deg s=6$ and $\deg w=4$.

Although the graded abelian groups are isomorphic in all degrees, the two rings are not isomorphic. The ring $`H^*(S^6 \times \mathbb{HP}^{\infty} ; \mathbb{Z})`$ contains a non-zero nilpotent element, $s$, of degree 6 (since $s^2=0$). In contrast, the ring $`\tilde{H}^*(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}/\mathbb{CP}^1; \mathbb{Z}) \cong (x^2) \subset \mathbb{Z}[x]`$ is a subring of a polynomial ring over an integral domain, and thus contains no non-zero nilpotent elements. This fundamental difference in ring properties proves they are not isomorphic.

# 4.
Show that if $H_n(X ; \mathbb{Z})$ is finitely generated and free for each $n$, then $`H^*(X ; \mathbb{Z}_p)`$ and $H^*(X ; \mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p$ are isomorphic as rings, so in particular the ring structure with $\mathbb{Z}$-coefficients determines the ring structure with $\mathbb{Z}_p$-coefficients.

**Proof**

Since $H_n(X ; \mathbb{Z})$ is finitely generated and free for each $n$, we can apply the universal coefficient theorem for cohomology, which states that there is a natural short exact sequence:

$0 \to \text{Ext}(H_{n-1}(X ; \mathbb{Z}), \mathbb{Z}_p) \to H^n(X ; \mathbb{Z}_p) \to \text{Hom}(H_n(X ; \mathbb{Z}), \mathbb{Z}_p) \to 0$

Since $H_{n-1}(X ; \mathbb{Z})$ is free, $\text{Ext}(H_{n-1}(X ; \mathbb{Z}), \mathbb{Z}_p) = 0$. Therefore, we have an isomorphism:

$H^n(X ; \mathbb{Z}_p) \cong \text{Hom}(H_n(X ; \mathbb{Z}), \mathbb{Z}_p)$

Since $H_n(X ; \mathbb{Z})$ is free and finitely generated, it is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}^r$ for some $r$. Thus, we have:

$H^n(X ; \mathbb{Z}_p) \cong \text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^r, \mathbb{Z}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^r \cong\mathbb{Z}^r \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p \cong H_n(X ; \mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p$

Since this isomorphism is natural, it respects the ring structure induced by the cup product. Therefore, we have an isomorphism of rings:

$H^n(X ; \mathbb{Z}_p) \cong H^n(X ; \mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p$

# 5.
Show that the cross product map $`H^*(X ; \mathbb{Z}) \otimes H^*(Y ; \mathbb{Z}) \rightarrow H^*(X \times Y ; \mathbb{Z})`$ is not an isomorphism if $X$ and $Y$ are infinite discrete sets.

**Proof**

The algebraic version of this problem is to prove that the natural linear map $\left( \prod_{I} \mathbb{Z} \right) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \left( \prod_{J} \mathbb{Z} \right) \to \prod_{I \times J} \mathbb{Z}$ is not surjective when the index sets $I$ and $J$ are infinite.

Let $I = J = \mathbb{N}$. Consider the infinite identity matrix $M \in \prod_{I \times J} \mathbb{Z}$, where $M_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$ (Kronecker delta).
An element in the image of the map is a finite sum of elementary tensors, which corresponds to a finite sum of rank-1 matrices. Specifically, if an element is in the image, it can be written as $\sum_{k=1}^N (x_{ki} y_{kj})_{i,j}$ for some finite $N$. This implies that the corresponding infinite matrix has finite rank (at most $N$).
However, the infinite identity matrix $M$ has infinite rank. Therefore, $M$ cannot be expressed as a finite sum of rank-1 matrices, and thus it is not in the image of the map. This demonstrates that the map is not surjective.
