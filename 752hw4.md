# 1.
Show that every covering space of an orientable manifold is an orientable manifold.

**Proof**

Let $p: \tilde{M} \to M$ be a covering map, where $M$ is an orientable $n$-manifold. To show $\tilde{M}$ is orientable, we need to demonstrate the existence of a consistent choice of generators for the local homology groups $`H_n(\tilde{M}, \tilde{M} \setminus \{\tilde{x}\}; \mathbb{Z})`$ for all $\tilde{x} \in \tilde{M}$.

Let $\tilde{x} \in \tilde{M}$ and let $x = p(\tilde{x})$. Since $p$ is a local homeomorphism, it restricts to a homeomorphism from a neighborhood $U$ of $\tilde{x}$ to a neighborhood $V$ of $x$. This induces a homeomorphism of pairs $`(U, U \setminus \{\tilde{x}\}) \to (V, V \setminus \{x\})`$, which in turn induces an isomorphism on homology. By the excision property, we have the following chain of isomorphisms:

$`H_n(\tilde{M}, \tilde{M} \setminus \{\tilde{x}\}; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H_n(U, U \setminus \{\tilde{x}\}; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H_n(V, V \setminus \{x\}; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H_n(M, M \setminus \{x\}; \mathbb{Z})`$

For any $n$-manifold, the local homology group $`H_n(X, X \setminus \{x\}; \mathbb{Z})`$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$. The orientability of $M$ means there exists a consistent choice of generators for these local homology groups across $M$, i.e., for every $x \in M$, there exists a closed ball $B \subset U_x \cong \mathbb{R}^n$ and a (generating) class $\mu_B \in H_n(M, M \backslash B ; \mathbb{Z})$ such that $\rho_y: H_n(M, M \backslash B ; \mathbb{Z}) \rightarrow H_n(M, M \backslash\{y\} ; \mathbb{Z})$ takes $\mu_B$ to $\mu_y$ for all $y \in B$. Since the covering map $p$ induces isomorphisms

$`p_*: H_n(\tilde{M}, \tilde{M} \setminus \{\tilde{x}\}; \mathbb{Z}) \to H_n(M, M \setminus \{p(\tilde{x})\}; \mathbb{Z})`$

for each $\tilde{x} \in \tilde{M}$, we can define a consistent choice of generators

$`\tilde{\mu}_{\tilde{x}} = p_*^{-1}(\mu_{p(\tilde{x})})`$

for $\tilde{M}$ by lifting the orientation from $M$ to $\tilde{M}$, thereby defining a global orientation for $\tilde{M}$. Therefore, $\tilde{M}$ is orientable.

# 2.
Given a covering space action of a group $G$ on an orientable manifold $M$ by orientation-preserving homeomorphisms, show that $M / G$ is also orientable.
# 3.
For a map $f: M \rightarrow N$ between connected closed orientable $n$-manifolds with fundamental classes $[M]$ and $[N]$, the degree of $f$ is defined to be the integer $d$ such that $f_*([M])=d[N]$, so the sign of the degree depends on the choice of fundamental classes. Show that for any connected closed orientable $n$-manifold $M$ there is a degree 1 map $M \rightarrow S^n$.
# 4.
Show that a $p$-sheeted covering space projection $M \rightarrow N$ has degree $p$, when $M$ and $N$ are connected closed orientable manifolds.
# 5.
Given two disjoint connected $n$-manifolds $M_1$ and $M_2$, a connected $n$-manifold $`M_1 \# M_2`$, their connected sum, can be constructed by deleting the interiors of closed $n$-balls $B_1 \subset M_1$ and $B_2 \subset M_2$ and identifying the resulting boundary spheres $\partial B_1$ and $\partial B_2$ via some homeomorphism between them. (Assume that each $B_i$ embeds nicely in a larger ball in $M_i$.)
- Show that if $M_1$ and $M_2$ are closed then there are isomorphisms

$`H_i\left(M_1 \# M_2 ; \mathbb{Z}\right) \simeq H_i\left(M_1 ; \mathbb{Z}\right) \oplus H_i\left(M_2 ; \mathbb{Z}\right), \text { for } 0<i<n,`$

with one exception: If both $M_1$ and $M_2$ are non-orientable, then the group $`H_{n-1}\left(M_1 \# M_2 ; \mathbb{Z}\right)`$ is obtained from $H_{n-1}\left(M_1 ; \mathbb{Z}\right) \oplus H_{n-1}\left(M_2 ; \mathbb{Z}\right)$ by replacing one of the two $\mathbb{Z}_2$-summands by a $\mathbb{Z}$-summand.
- Show that $`\chi\left(M_1 \# M_2\right)=\chi\left(M_1\right)+\chi\left(M_2\right)-\chi\left(S^n\right)`$ if $M_1$ and $M_2$ are closed.
