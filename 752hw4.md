# 1.
Show that every covering space of an orientable manifold is an orientable manifold.

**Proof**

Let $p: \tilde{M} \to M$ be a covering map, where $M$ is an orientable $n$-manifold. To show $\tilde{M}$ is orientable, we need to demonstrate the existence of a consistent choice of generators for the local homology groups $`H_n(\tilde{M}, \tilde{M} \setminus \{\tilde{x}\}; \mathbb{Z})`$ for all $\tilde{x} \in \tilde{M}$.

Let $\tilde{x} \in \tilde{M}$ and let $x = p(\tilde{x})$. Since $p$ is a local homeomorphism, it restricts to a homeomorphism from a neighborhood $U$ of $\tilde{x}$ to a neighborhood $V$ of $x$. This induces a homeomorphism of pairs $`(U, U \setminus \{\tilde{x}\}) \to (V, V \setminus \{x\})`$, which in turn induces an isomorphism on homology. By the excision property, we have the following chain of isomorphisms:

$`H_n(\tilde{M}, \tilde{M} \setminus \{\tilde{x}\}; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H_n(U, U \setminus \{\tilde{x}\}; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H_n(V, V \setminus \{x\}; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H_n(M, M \setminus \{x\}; \mathbb{Z})`$

For any $n$-manifold, the local homology group $`H_n(X, X \setminus \{x\}; \mathbb{Z})`$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$. The orientability of $M$ means there exists a consistent choice of generators for these local homology groups across $M$, i.e., for every $x \in M$, there exists a closed ball $B \subset U_x \cong \mathbb{R}^n$ and a (generating) class $\mu_B \in H_n(M, M \setminus B ; \mathbb{Z})$ such that

$`\rho_y: H_n(M, M \setminus B ; \mathbb{Z}) \rightarrow H_n(M, M \setminus\{y\} ; \mathbb{Z})`$

takes $\mu_B$ to $\mu_y$ for all $y \in B$. Since the covering map $p$ induces isomorphisms

$`p_*: H_n(\tilde{M}, \tilde{M} \setminus \{\tilde{x}\}; \mathbb{Z}) \to H_n(M, M \setminus \{p(\tilde{x})\}; \mathbb{Z})`$

for each $\tilde{x} \in \tilde{M}$, we can define a consistent choice of generators

$`\tilde{\mu}_{\tilde{x}} = p_*^{-1}(\mu_{p(\tilde{x})})`$

for $\tilde{M}$ by lifting the orientation from $M$ to $\tilde{M}$. This defines a global orientation, as the local orientation on a ball $B \subset M$ can be lifted to each component of its preimage $p^{-1}(B)$. Therefore, $\tilde{M}$ is orientable.

# 2.
Given a covering space action of a group $G$ on an orientable manifold $M$ by orientation-preserving homeomorphisms, show that $M / G$ is also orientable.

**Proof**

Let $p: M \to M/G$ be the quotient map. To show $M/G$ is orientable, we must define a consistent choice of local orientations. A local orientation at a point $y \in M/G$ is a generator of the local homology group $`H_n(M/G, M/G \setminus \{y\}; \mathbb{Z})`$.
Let $y \in M/G$. Choose any point $x \in p^{-1}(y) \subset M$. Since $p$ is a covering map, there exists a neighborhood $U$ of $x$ in $M$ such that the restriction

$p|_U: U \to p(U)$

is a homeomorphism. $p(U)$ is a neighborhood of $y$ in $M/G$.

Since $M$ is orientable, there is a generator $`\mu_x \in H_n(M, M \setminus \{x\}; \mathbb{Z})`$. By excision, this corresponds to a generator of $`H_n(U, U \setminus \{x\}; \mathbb{Z})`$, which we also denote by $\mu_x$. We can define a generator $\mu_y$ for $`H_n(M/G, M/G \setminus \{y\}; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H_n(p(U), p(U) \setminus \{y\}; \mathbb{Z})`$ by setting:
$`\mu_y = (p|_U)_*(\mu_x)`$

We must show this definition is independent of the choice of $x$ in the fiber $p^{-1}(y)$. Let $x' \in p^{-1}(y)$ be another point. Then $x' = g(x)$ for some $g \in G$. Since the action of $G$ on $M$ is by orientation-preserving homeomorphisms, the induced map on homology $`g_*: H_n(M, M \setminus \{x\}; \mathbb{Z}) \to H_n(M, M \setminus \{x'\}; \mathbb{Z})`$ sends the generator $\mu_x$ to the generator $\mu_{x'}$. That is, $\mu_{x'} = g_*(\mu_x)$.

Using $x'$ to define the orientation at $y$ gives a generator $`\mu'_y = (p|_{U'})_*(\mu_{x'})`$, where $U'$ is a neighborhood of $x'$ on which $p$ is a homeomorphism. We can choose $U' = g(U)$. Then:

$`\mu'_y = (p|_{g(U)})_*(g_*(\mu_x)) = (p|_{g(U)} \circ g)_*(\mu_x)`$

Since $p \circ g = p$, the restriction to $U$ gives $p|_{g(U)} \circ g = p|_U$. Therefore:
$`\mu'_y = (p|_U)_*(\mu_x) = \mu_y`$

This shows that the choice of generator $\mu_y$ is well-defined, independent of the point chosen in the fiber.

The local consistency of this orientation on $M/G$ follows from the local consistency of the orientation on $M$ and the fact that $p$ is a local homeomorphism. Thus, $M/G$ is orientable.

# 3.
For a map $f: M \rightarrow N$ between connected closed orientable $n$-manifolds with fundamental classes $[M]$ and $[N]$, the degree of $f$ is defined to be the integer $d$ such that $f_*([M])=d[N]$, so the sign of the degree depends on the choice of fundamental classes. Show that for any connected closed orientable $n$-manifold $M$ there is a degree 1 map $M \rightarrow S^n$.
# 4.
Show that a $p$-sheeted covering space projection $M \rightarrow N$ has degree $p$, when $M$ and $N$ are connected closed orientable manifolds.

**Proof**

Let $p: M \rightarrow N$ be the covering map. Since $N$ is orientable, its covering space $M$ is also orientable. We choose an orientation for $N$ and then define an orientation on $M$ by lifting $N$'s orientation via the local isomorphisms $`p_*: H_n(M, M \setminus \{x\}) \to H_n(N, N \setminus \{p(x)\})`$. By this construction, $p$ is an orientation-preserving map. The degree of $p$ is the sum of the local degrees over the fiber $p^{-1}(y)$ for any $y \in N$. Since $p$ is orientation-preserving, the local degree at each point in $p^{-1}(y)$ is $+1$. As this is a $p$-sheeted covering, the fiber contains exactly $p$ points. Therefore, the degree of $p$ is the sum of $p$ ones, which is $p$.

# 5.
Given two disjoint connected $n$-manifolds $M_1$ and $M_2$, a connected $n$-manifold $`M_1 \# M_2`$, their connected sum, can be constructed by deleting the interiors of closed $n$-balls $B_1 \subset M_1$ and $B_2 \subset M_2$ and identifying the resulting boundary spheres $\partial B_1$ and $\partial B_2$ via some homeomorphism between them. (Assume that each $B_i$ embeds nicely in a larger ball in $M_i$.)
- Show that if $M_1$ and $M_2$ are closed then there are isomorphisms

    $`H_i\left(M_1 \# M_2 ; \mathbb{Z}\right) \simeq H_i\left(M_1 ; \mathbb{Z}\right) \oplus H_i\left(M_2 ; \mathbb{Z}\right), \text { for } 0<i<n,`$
    
    with one exception: If both $M_1$ and $M_2$ are non-orientable, then the group $`H_{n-1}\left(M_1 \# M_2 ; \mathbb{Z}\right)`$ is obtained from $H_{n-1}\left(M_1 ; \mathbb{Z}\right) \oplus H_{n-1}\left(M_2 ; \mathbb{Z}\right)$ by replacing one of the two $\mathbb{Z}_2$-summands by a $\mathbb{Z}$-summand.

**Proof**

- By Mayer-Vietoris sequence:

$`H_i(M_1 \# M_2; \mathbb{Z}) \simeq H_i(M_1; \mathbb{Z}) \oplus H_i(M_2; \mathbb{Z}), \text { for } 0<i<n,`$
- Show that $`\chi\left(M_1 \# M_2\right)=\chi\left(M_1\right)+\chi\left(M_2\right)-\chi\left(S^n\right)`$ if $M_1$ and $M_2$ are closed.

**Proof**

The Euler characteristic is the alternating sum of Betti numbers:

$`\chi(M_1 \# M_2) = \sum_{i=0}^n (-1)^i b_i(M_1 \# M_2) = \sum_{i=0}^n (-1)^i (b_i(M_1) + b_i(M_2) - \delta_{i,0} - \delta_{i,n}) = \chi(M_1) + \chi(M_2) - 1 - (-1)^n = \chi(M_1) + \chi(M_2) - \chi(S^n)`$
