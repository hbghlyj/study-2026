# Problem A
Let $E \supset F$ be an algebraic extension. Choose an algebraic closure $\bar{F}$ of $F$ such that $E \subset \bar{F}$. Prove that the following conditions are equivalent:
1. $E$ is the splitting field of a family of polynomials $ `S \subset F[x] \setminus \{0\}` $;
2. For any $F$-homomorphism $\phi: E \rightarrow \bar{F}, \phi(E) \subset E$;
3. The minimal polynomial of every $a \in E$ over $F$ splits over $E$.
(The trick to showing that 2 implies 3 is to consider $E$ as an extension of $F(a)$.)
# Problem B
Let $F$ be a perfect field. Prove that all finite extensions $E \supset F$ are perfect as well.
From this, it easily follows that all algebraic extensions of $F$ are perfect, but you do not need to prove this.

Hint: the most direct way to prove this is to consider the map $x \mapsto x^p: E \rightarrow E$ as a map between $F$-vector spaces, where $p>0$ is the characteristic.
This is not going to work if you consider $E$ as a vector space over $F$ in the obvious way (why?), so you'll have to be clever.
