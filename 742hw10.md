# 2.3
Construct a splitting field for $X^5-2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. What is its degree over $\mathbb{Q}$?

**Proof**
Let $\zeta$ be a primitive 5th root of unity.
The polynomial $f(X) = X^5-2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein's criterion with $p=2$, as $2$ divides all non-leading coefficients and $2^2$ does not divide the constant term. Thus, $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[5]{2}):\mathbb{Q}]=5$.
The cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_5(X) = X^4+X^3+X^2+X+1$ is irreducible. Consider $g(X) = \Phi_5(X+1) = \frac{(X+1)^5-1}{(X+1)-1} = X^4 + 5X^3 + 10X^2 + 10X + 5$. Since $5$ divides $5, 10, 10, 5$ and $5^2 \nmid 5$, $g(X)$ is irreducible by Eisenstein's criterion with $p=5$, implying $\Phi_5(X)$ is irreducible. Thus, $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}]=4$.
The splitting field is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[5]{2}, \zeta)$. Since $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[5]{2}):\mathbb{Q}]=5$ and $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}]=4$ are coprime, the degree of the composite field is $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 5 \times 4 = 20$.
# 2.6
Let $f(X)$ be an irreducible polynomial in $F[X]$, where $F$ has characteristic $p$. Show that $f(X)$ can be written $f(X)=g(X^{p^e})$ where $g(X)$ is irreducible and separable.
Deduce that every root of $f(X)$ has the same multiplicity $p^e$ in any splitting field.

**Proof**
If $f(X)$ is separable, we take $e=0$ and $g(X)=f(X)$. If $f(X)$ is not separable, then its derivative $f'(X)$ must be the zero polynomial because $f$ is irreducible and $\deg(f') < \deg(f)$. This implies $f(X) = f_1(X^p)$ for some $f_1 \in F[X]$. Since $f$ is irreducible, $f_1$ is also irreducible. By induction, $f(X) = g(X^{p^e})$ for some $e \geq 0$ where $g'(X) \neq 0$. Since $g$ is irreducible and $g'(X) \neq 0$, $g$ is separable. In a splitting field, if $\alpha$ is a root of $g$, then $X^{p^e} - \alpha = (X - \beta)^{p^e}$ where $\beta^{p^e} = \alpha$. Since $g$ is separable, its roots are distinct, so each root of $f$ has multiplicity exactly $p^e$.
# Problem A
Let $E \supset F$ be an algebraic extension. Choose an algebraic closure $\bar{F}$ of $F$ such that $E \subset \bar{F}$. Prove that the following conditions are equivalent:
1. $E$ is the splitting field of a family of polynomials $`S \subset F[x] \setminus \{0\}`$;
2. For any $F$-homomorphism $\phi: E \rightarrow \bar{F}, \phi(E) \subset E$;
3. The minimal polynomial of every $a \in E$ over $F$ splits over $E$.
(The trick to showing that 2 implies 3 is to consider $E$ as an extension of $F(a)$.)

**Proof**
($1 \implies 2$) Since $E$ is the splitting field of a family of polynomials $S \subset F[x] \setminus \{0\}$, $E$ is generated over $F$ by the set $R$ of all roots of polynomials in $S$. An $F$-homomorphism $\phi: E \to \bar{F}$ maps roots of a polynomial $f \in F[x]$ to roots of the same polynomial. Thus $\phi(R) \subseteq R$, which implies $\phi(E) = \phi(F(R)) = F(\phi(R)) \subseteq F(R) = E$.

($2 \implies 3$) Let $b\in\bar{F}$ be any root of the minimal polynomial $m_a$ of $a$. There is an $F$-homomorphism $\phi$ mapping $a$ to $b$. By (2), $b\in E$. So $m_a$ splits.

($3 \implies 1$) take $S$ be minimal polynomial of all elements of $F$
