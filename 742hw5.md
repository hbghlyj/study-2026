# Problem A
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an arbitrary category. Consider the category of functors $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C}, \text{Sets})$. Prove that this category admits products:
for any family of functors $F_\alpha \in \mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C}, \text{Sets})$, the product exists.

# Problem B
Continuing with the previous problem, show that the Yoneda embedding

$a \mapsto h_a$

sends coproducts in $\mathcal{C}$ to products in $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C}, \text{Sets})$. (Assuming coproducts in $\mathcal{C}$ exist.)

# Problem C
More generally, suppose $a_\alpha$ is an arbitrary family of elements of $\mathcal{C}$. Show that the coproduct $\coprod a_\alpha$ exists iff the functor $\prod h_{a_\alpha}$ is representable.
("representable" means that it is isomorphic to $h_b$ for some object $b$.)

# Problem D
Consider the following functor from the category of sets to itself:

$`X \mapsto X \sqcup\{*\},`$

where $`\{*\}`$ denotes the set with a single object. Is this functor representable? Why or why not?
