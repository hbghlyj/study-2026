1. Let $A^\alpha{ }_\beta(t)$ be a smooth matrix-valued function of $t \in \mathbb{R}$. Prove that any local solution $\left(s^\alpha(t)\right)$ of the ODE
  
    $\frac{d s^\alpha}{d t}+A^\alpha{ }_\beta s^\beta=0$
    
    extends to all of $\mathbb{R}$. You may assume the standard local existence and uniqueness theorem for ODEs, see e.g. the 761 notes, Theorem 19.1.

2. (a) Define a connection $A$ on the trivial line bundle over $\mathbb{R}^2$ by
    
    $A_1(x, y)=y, \quad A_2(x, y)=x .$
    
    Show (explicitly) that $A$ is globally gauge-equivalent (i.e. isomorphic) to the product connection.

   (b) Instead let
    
    $A_1(x, y)=-y, \quad A_2(x, y)=x .$
    
    Show that $A$ is not gauge-equivalent to the product connection.

3. Suppose that $\sigma \in \Gamma(\mathrm{End} E)$ is an automorphism of $E$. Given a connection $A$, recall that $\sigma$ acts on $A$ by

    $(\nabla_{\sigma(A)})_X(s) := \sigma((\nabla_A)_X(\sigma^{-1}s))$
    
    Let $x_0 \in M$. Show that for a loop $\gamma$ based at $M$, we have
    
    $P^{\sigma(A), \gamma}=\sigma_{x_0} \cdot P^{A, \gamma} \cdot \sigma_{x_0}^{-1}: E_{x_0} \rightarrow E_{x_0} .$

   **Proof**

   The proof strategy relies on the uniqueness of parallel transport. If a section satisfies the following conditions:

   1. It has a zero covariant derivative under the new connection.

      To show $\tilde{s}(t)$ is parallel under $\tilde{A}$, we apply the transformed covariant derivative. Using the definition of the transformed connection $\tilde{\nabla}$ and that $\tilde{s}(t) = \sigma(\gamma(t))s(t)$:

      $`\tilde{\nabla}_{\dot{\gamma}} \tilde{s}(t) = \sigma(\gamma(t)) \left( \nabla_{\dot{\gamma}} (\sigma(\gamma(t))^{-1} \tilde{s}(t)) \right) = \sigma(\gamma(t)) \left( \nabla_{\dot{\gamma}} s(t) \right)`$

      Since $s(t)$ is parallel under $A$, we know $\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}} s(t) = 0$, so:

      $`\tilde{\nabla}_{\dot{\gamma}} \tilde{s}(t) = \sigma(\gamma(t)) \cdot 0 = 0`$
   3. It starts with the correct initial value. We check this for a candidate section $\tilde{s}(t) = \sigma(\gamma(t))s(t)$, where $s(0) = \sigma(\gamma(0))^{-1}v$ for some $v \in E_{x_0}$. At $t=0$:
       
       $\tilde{s}(0) = \sigma(\gamma(0))s(0) = \sigma(\gamma(0))(\sigma(\gamma(0))^{-1} v) = v$
       
       This shows the section starts with the initial vector $v$.

   Then, by uniqueness, it must be the section that defines parallel transport.
