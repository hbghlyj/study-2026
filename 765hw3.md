1. Let $A^\alpha{ }_\beta(t)$ be a smooth matrix-valued function of $t \in \mathbb{R}$. Prove that any local solution $\left(s^\alpha(t)\right)$ of the ODE
  
    $\frac{d s^\alpha}{d t}+A^\alpha{ }_\beta s^\beta=0$
    
    extends to all of $\mathbb{R}$. You may assume the standard local existence and uniqueness theorem for ODEs, see e.g. the 761 notes, Theorem 19.1.

2. (a) Define a connection $A$ on the trivial line bundle over $\mathbb{R}^2$ by
    
    $A_1(x, y)=y, \quad A_2(x, y)=x .$
    
    Show (explicitly) that $A$ is globally gauge-equivalent (i.e. isomorphic) to the product connection.

    **Proof**

    Gauge transformations act on a connection 1-form $A$ by adding the exterior derivative of a smooth real-valued function. Specifically, under a gauge transformation parameterized by $\lambda \in C^{\infty}(M, \mathbb{R})$, the transformed connection is given by $A' = A + d\lambda$.
    
    Two connections are gauge-equivalent if one can be obtained from the other through such a transformation. A connection $A$ is gauge-equivalent to the trivial connection $A = 0$ if and only if there exists a smooth function $\lambda$ such that $A = d\lambda$, meaning $A$ is an exact 1-form.
    
    $`A = y \, dx + x \, dy = d(xy)`$

   (b) Instead let
    
    $A_1(x, y)=-y, \quad A_2(x, y)=x .$
    
    Show that $A$ is not gauge-equivalent to the product connection.

    **Proof**

    $`dA = d(-y \, dx + x \, dy) =  2dx \wedge dy \ne 0`$, so $A$ is not an exact 1-form and is therefore not gauge-equivalent to the trivial connection $A = 0$.

3. Suppose that $\sigma \in \Gamma(\mathrm{End} E)$ is an automorphism of $E$. Given a connection $A$, recall that $\sigma$ acts on $A$ by

    $(\nabla_{\sigma(A)})_X(s) := \sigma((\nabla_A)_X(\sigma^{-1}s))$
    
    Let $x_0 \in M$. Show that for a loop $\gamma$ based at $M$, we have
    
    $P^{\sigma(A), \gamma}=\sigma_{x_0} \cdot P^{A, \gamma} \cdot \sigma_{x_0}^{-1}: E_{x_0} \rightarrow E_{x_0} .$

   **Proof**
   
   Let $s(t)$ be the parallel section of $\nabla_A$ starting at $s(0) = \sigma_{x_0}^{-1}v$ for some $v \in E_{x_0}$.

   The proof strategy relies on the uniqueness of parallel transport. We check that the candidate section $\tilde{s}(t) = \sigma(\gamma(t))s(t)$ satisfies the following conditions:
   1. It starts with the correct initial value. At $t=0$:
       
       $\tilde{s}(0) = \sigma(\gamma(0))s(0) = \sigma(\gamma(0))(\sigma(\gamma(0))^{-1} v) = v$
       
       This shows the section starts with the initial vector $v$.
   2. It has a zero covariant derivative under the new connection.

      To show $\tilde{s}(t)$ is parallel under $\tilde{A}$, we apply the transformed covariant derivative. Using the definition of the transformed connection $\tilde{\nabla}$ and that $\tilde{s}(t) = \sigma(\gamma(t))s(t)$:

      $`(\nabla_{\sigma(A)})_{\dot{\gamma}} \tilde{s}(t) = \sigma(\gamma(t)) \left( \nabla_{\dot{\gamma}} (\sigma(\gamma(t))^{-1} \tilde{s}(t)) \right) = \sigma(\gamma(t)) \left( \nabla_{\dot{\gamma}} s(t) \right)`$

      Since $s(t)$ is parallel under $A$, we know $\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}} s(t) = 0$, so:

      $`(\nabla_{\sigma(A)})_{\dot{\gamma}} \tilde{s}(t) = \sigma(\gamma(t)) (0) = 0`$

    Then, by uniqueness, $\tilde{s}(t)$ is the parallel transport of $v$ along $\gamma$ with respect to $\nabla_{\sigma(A)}$. Thus, the parallel transport map is given by $P^{\sigma(A), \gamma}(v) = \tilde{s}(1)$.
    
    We have:
    $P^{\sigma(A), \gamma}(v) = \tilde{s}(1) = \sigma(\gamma(1))s(1) = \sigma_{x_0} s(1)$.
    
    Since $s(t)$ is the parallel transport for $\nabla_A$ with starting vector $s(0) = \sigma_{x_0}^{-1}v$, we have $s(1) = P^{A, \gamma}(s(0)) = P^{A, \gamma}(\sigma_{x_0}^{-1}v)$.
    
    Combining these, we get:
    $P^{\sigma(A), \gamma}(v) = \sigma_{x_0} P^{A, \gamma}(\sigma_{x_0}^{-1}v) = (\sigma_{x_0} \circ P^{A, \gamma} \circ \sigma_{x_0}^{-1})(v)$.
    
    As this holds for all $v \in E_{x_0}$, the desired identity follows.
4. Recall the local formula for the curvature
  
   $$F_{i j}=\frac{\partial A_j}{\partial x^i}-\frac{\partial A_i}{\partial x^j}+\left[A_i, A_j\right],$$

   where we view $A_i=\left(A_{i \beta}^\alpha\right)$ and $F_{i j}=\left(F_{i j}{ }^\alpha{ }_\beta\right)$ as matrix-valued functions.

   Check directly using the transformation rule for $A_i$ (from class) that $F_{i j}$ transforms under a change of frame as:
   
   $$F_{i j}'=\sigma \cdot F_{i j} \cdot \sigma^{-1}$$


   **Proof**

    Let $A_i' = \sigma A_i \sigma^{-1} - (\partial_i \sigma) \sigma^{-1}$ be the transformed connection 1-form under the gauge transformation $\sigma$. We want to show that the curvature transforms as $F_{ij}' = \sigma F_{ij} \sigma^{-1}$.
    
    We compute $F_{ij}'$ using the definition of curvature:
    
    $`\begin{aligned}
   F'_{ij} &= \partial_i A'_j - \partial_j A'_i + [A'_i, A'_j] \\
   &= \partial_i(\sigma A_j \sigma^{-1} - (\partial_j\sigma)\sigma^{-1}) - \partial_j(\sigma A_i \sigma^{-1} - (\partial_i\sigma)\sigma^{-1}) + [\sigma A_i \sigma^{-1} - (\partial_i\sigma)\sigma^{-1}, \sigma A_j \sigma^{-1} - (\partial_j\sigma)\sigma^{-1}]
   \end{aligned}`$

    Expanding this expression and using the fact that the derivative of the inverse matrix is given by $`D(\sigma^{-1}) = -\sigma^{-1} D(\sigma) \sigma^{-1}`$, we find that the terms involving derivatives of $\sigma$ cancel out, the terms involving only $A$ and its derivatives combine to $\sigma F_{ij} \sigma^{-1}$. All other terms, which involve $\sigma$ and its derivatives, cancel out. For example, $(\partial_i\sigma)A_j\sigma^{-1}$ from $\partial_i A_j'$ cancels with $- (\partial_i\sigma)A_j\sigma^{-1}$ from the commutator term. Similarly, all other mixed terms cancel in pairs.

   $`\begin{aligned}&((\partial_i σ) A_j + σ \partial_i (A_j)) σ^{-1} - (σ A_j σ^{-1} (\partial_i σ) σ^{-1}) -
        (\partial_i (\partial_j σ) σ^{-1} - ((\partial_j σ) σ^{-1} (\partial_i σ) σ^{-1})) \\&-
      (((\partial_j σ) A_i + σ \partial_j (A_i)) σ^{-1} - (σ A_i σ^{-1} (\partial_j σ) σ^{-1}) -
        (\partial_i (\partial_j σ) σ^{-1} - ((\partial_i σ) σ^{-1} (\partial_j σ) σ^{-1}))) \\&+
    (σ A_i A_j σ^{-1} - (\partial_i σ) A_j σ^{-1} -
        (σ A_i σ^{-1} (\partial_j σ) σ^{-1} - (\partial_i σ) σ^{-1} (\partial_j σ) σ^{-1}) \\&-
      (σ A_j A_i σ^{-1} - (\partial_j σ) A_i σ^{-1} -
        (σ A_j σ^{-1} (\partial_i σ) σ^{-1} - (\partial_j σ) σ^{-1} (\partial_i σ) σ^{-1}))) =
  σ (\partial_i (A_j) - \partial_j (A_i) + (A_i A_j - A_j A_i)) σ^{-1}\end{aligned}`$

    $`F_{ij}' = \sigma (\partial_i A_j - \partial_j A_i + [A_i, A_j]) \sigma^{-1} = \sigma F_{ij} \sigma^{-1}`$
    
    Thus, we have shown that the curvature transforms as claimed under a change of frame.
