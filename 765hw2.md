1. Let $B \in \Gamma(\mathrm{End} E)$ be a section, with local components $B^\alpha{ }_\beta$.
    
    Suppose that $\sigma=\left(\sigma^\alpha{ }_\beta\right)$ is a change-of-frame. Show that, in matrix notation, the components of $B$ in the new frame are given by
    
    $B'=\sigma \cdot B \cdot \sigma^{-1} .$
    
    **Proof**
    
    Switch to a new basis using a transformation matrix $g$. This matrix $g$ is defined such that it takes a vector $x'$ (expressed in the new basis) and converts it back to $x$ (the original basis):$x=gx'$Think of $g$ as the "New-to-Old" translator.Find the Inverse MappingTo go the other way—from the original basis to the new one—you use the inverse, $g^{-1}$. If $y$ is a result in the original basis, its new-basis version $y'$ is:$y'=g^{-1}y$This is the "Old-to-New" translator.Chain the OperationsTo find how the operator acts entirely within the new basis (finding $M'$ such that $y'=M'x'$), you follow the physical path of the vector:Start with your new-basis vector: $x'$Step 1: Translate it to the original basis: $gx'$Step 2: Apply the original transformation $M$: $M(gx')$Step 3: Translate that result back to the new basis: $g^{-1}(Mgx')$Identify the New MatrixThe full sequence is $y'=(g^{-1}Mg)x'$. By comparing this to the definition $y'=M'x'$, we see that:$M'=g^{-1}Mg$ This is "homogeneous" because $M'$ is purely a re-scaling and rotation of $M$ by $g$.
