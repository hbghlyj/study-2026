# 1
Let $f: X \rightarrow Y$ be a homotopy equivalence. Let $Z$ be any other space. Show that $f$ induces bijections:

$`f_*:[Z, X] \rightarrow[Z, Y] \text { and } f^*:[Y, Z] \rightarrow[X, Z]`$

where $[A, B]$ denotes the set of homotopy classes of maps from the space $A$ to $B$.

**Proof.** Since $f$ is a homotopy equivalence, there exists a map $g: Y \rightarrow X$ such that $g \circ f \simeq id_X$ and $f \circ g \simeq id_Y$. The induced maps $`f_*`$ and $f^*$ are defined as follows:
- $f_*: [Z, X] \rightarrow [Z, Y]$ is given by $[h] \mapsto [f \circ h]$ for any map $h: Z \rightarrow X$.
- $f^*: [Y, Z] \rightarrow [X, Z]$ is given by $[k] \mapsto [k \circ f]$ for any map $k: Y \rightarrow Z$.

To show that $`f_*`$ is a bijection, we observe that $g$ induces a map $`g_*: [Z, Y] \rightarrow [Z, X]`$ defined by $[k] \mapsto [g \circ k]$. Since $g \circ f \simeq id_X$, we have $`g_* \circ f_*([h]) = [g \circ f \circ h] = [h]`$, so $g_* \circ f_* = id_{[Z, X]}$. Similarly, $f \circ g \simeq id_Y$ implies $f_* \circ g_* = id_{[Z, Y]}$.

Similarly, $`f^*`$ is a bijection with inverse $`g^*: [X, Z] \rightarrow [Y, Z]`$ given by $[l] \mapsto [l \circ g]$. Indeed, $`f^* \circ g^*([l]) = [l \circ g \circ f] = [l]`$ and $`g^* \circ f^*([k]) = [k \circ f \circ g] = [k]`$, using the homotopies $g \circ f \simeq id_X$ and $f \circ g \simeq id_Y$.

Find examples of spaces $X$ and $Y$ which have the same homology groups, cohomology groups, and cohomology rings, but with different homotopy groups.

**Example 1.** Consider the spaces $X = S^3$ and $Y = P$, where $P$ is the Poincaré homology sphere. Both $X$ and $Y$ have homology groups $H_0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$, $H_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}$, and $H_n = 0$ for all other $n$. Their cohomology groups are also isomorphic, and since all products of positive-degree elements must vanish for degree reasons, their cohomology rings are isomorphic. However, their homotopy groups differ: $X$ is simply-connected ($\pi_1(X) = 0$), while $Y$ has a non-trivial fundamental group ($\pi_1(Y) \cong I_{120}$, the binary icosahedral group).

# 3
Use homotopy groups in order to show that there is no retraction $\mathbb{R P}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R P}^k$ if $n>k>0$.

**Proof.** Assume for contradiction that there exists a retraction $r: \mathbb{R P}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R P}^k$ with $n > k > 0$. A retraction induces a surjective homomorphism $r_*: \pi_q(\mathbb{R P}^n) \rightarrow \pi_q(\mathbb{R P}^k)$ for all $q \ge 1$. If $k=1$, then $\pi_1(\mathbb{R P}^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ for $n \ge 2$, while $\pi_1(\mathbb{R P}^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. There is no surjection from $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ to $\mathbb{Z}$. If $n > k > 1$, then $\pi_k(\mathbb{R P}^n) \cong \pi_k(S^n) = 0$ since $k < n$, but $\pi_k(\mathbb{R P}^k) \cong \pi_k(S^k) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. There is no surjection from $0$ to $\mathbb{Z}$. In both cases, we reach a contradiction.

# 4
Show that an $n$-connected, $n$-dimensional CW complex is contractible.

**Proof.** Since $X$ is $n$-connected, $\pi_k(X) = 0$ for $k \le n$. If $X$ were not contractible, let $i$ be the smallest integer such that $\pi_i(X) \neq 0$. By the Hurewicz theorem, $\pi_i(X) \cong H_i(X)$. However, since $X$ is an $n$-dimensional CW complex, $H_i(X) = 0$ for all $i > n$. This contradiction shows that $\pi_k(X) = 0$ for all $k$. By Whitehead's theorem, a CW complex with all vanishing homotopy groups is contractible.

# 5 (Extension Lemma)

Given a CW pair $(X, A)$ and a map $f: A \rightarrow Y$ with $Y$ path-connected, show that $f$ can be extended to a map $X \rightarrow Y$ if $\pi_{n-1}(Y)=0$ for all $n$ such that $X \setminus A$ has cells of dimension $n$.

**Proof.** We construct the extension inductively on $X_n = A \cup X^n$. For $n=0$, extend $f$ to $X_0$ by mapping $0$-cells in $X \setminus A$ to points in $Y$. Assume $f$ is extended to $f_{n-1}: X_{n-1} \rightarrow Y$. Each $n$-cell $e^n$ in $X \setminus A$ is attached via a map $\varphi: S^{n-1} \rightarrow X^{n-1} \subseteq X_{n-1}$. The composition $f_{n-1} \circ \varphi: S^{n-1} \rightarrow Y$ is nullhomotopic since $\pi_{n-1}(Y)=0$, so it extends to the $n$-disk $D^n$. This defines a continuous map $f_n: X_n \rightarrow Y$. The final map $f: X \rightarrow Y$ is continuous because $X$ has the weak topology with respect to the subspaces $X_n$.

# 6
Show that a CW complex retracts onto any contractible subcomplex. (Hint: Use the above extension lemma.)

**Proof.** Let $X$ be a CW complex and $A \subseteq X$ be a contractible subcomplex. Since $A$ is contractible, $\pi_k(A) = 0$ for all $k \ge 0$. By the extension lemma, the identity map $id_A: A \rightarrow A$ can be extended to a map $r: X \rightarrow A$. Since $r$ is an extension of $id_A$, we have $r|_A = id_A$, which by definition means $r$ is a retraction of $X$ onto $A$.
# 7
Use cellular approximation to show that the $n$-skeletons of homotopy equivalent CW complexes without cells of dimension $n+1$ are also homotopy equivalent.

**Proof.** Let $X$ and $Y$ be homotopy equivalent CW complexes without cells of dimension $n+1$. Let $f: X \rightarrow Y$ and $g: Y \rightarrow X$ be homotopy equivalences. By cellular approximation, we can homotope $f$ and $g$ to cellular maps $f': X \rightarrow Y$ and $g': Y \rightarrow X$. The restrictions $`f'|_{X^n}: X^n \rightarrow Y^n`$ and $`g'|_{Y^n}: Y^n \rightarrow X^n`$ are well-defined cellular maps. Since $g' \circ f' \simeq g \circ f \simeq id_X$ and both $g' \circ f'$ and $id_X$ are cellular, there exists a homotopy $H: X \times I \rightarrow X$ between them. By cellular approximation we may assume $H$ is cellular. Because $X$ has no cells of dimension $n+1$, $X^{n+1} = X^n$, so the cellularity of $H$ implies $H(X^n \times I) \subseteq X^{n+1} = X^n$. Thus $`H|_{X^n \times I}`$ is a homotopy between $`g'|_{Y^n} \circ f'|_{X^n}`$ and $id_{X^n}$. Similarly, $`f'|_{X^n} \circ g'|_{Y^n} \simeq id_{Y^n}`$, showing that $X^n$ and $Y^n$ are homotopy equivalent.
# 8
Show that a closed simply-connected 3-manifold is homotopy equivalent to $S^3$. (Hint: Use Poincaré Duality, and also the fact that closed manifolds are homotopy equivalent to CW complexes.)

**Proof.** Let $M$ be a closed simply-connected 3-manifold. Since $M$ is a closed manifold, it is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex.
By Hurewicz's theorem, since $M$ is simply-connected, $H_1(M) = 0$.
Since $M$ is simply-connected, it is orientable. By Poincaré Duality, $H_3(M) \cong H^0(M) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $H_2(M) \cong H^1(M)$. Since $H_1(M) = 0$, the Universal Coefficient Theorem implies $H^1(M) = 0$, so $H_2(M) = 0$. The homology groups of $M$ thus match those of $S^3$.
By Hurewicz's theorem again, $\pi_2(M) = 0$ and $\pi_3(M) \cong H_3(M) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. A generator of $\pi_3(M)$ corresponds to a map $f: S^3 \rightarrow M$ that induces an isomorphism on $H_3$.
By the Whitehead theorem, since $f$ induces isomorphisms on all homotopy groups between simply-connected CW complexes, it is a homotopy equivalence. Therefore, $M$ is homotopy equivalent to $S^3$.
