# 5
Suppose that a surface $S \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ contains a straight line $L$. Show that the Gauss curvature is nonpositive at all points along $L$.

**Proof**
Let $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$ be the principal curvatures of the surface $S$ at a point $p$ on the line $L$. The second fundamental form of the surface at $p$ can be expressed in terms of these principal curvatures as:
$$\text{II} = \kappa_1 du^2 + \kappa_2 dv^2,$$
where $du$ and $dv$ are the differentials of the local coordinates in the principal directions. Since $L$ is a straight line, the normal curvature in the direction of $L$ is zero. The principal curvatures, $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$, are the extremal values of the normal curvature. Thus, one principal curvature must be non-positive and the other non-negative (i.e., $\kappa_1 \le 0 \le \kappa_2$, assuming $\kappa_1 \le \kappa_2$). Therefore, the Gauss curvature $K = \kappa_1 \cdot \kappa_2$ must be nonpositive.
