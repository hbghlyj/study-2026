# 1.
Show that if $M^n$ is connected, non-compact manifold, then $H_i(M ; \mathbb{Z})=0$ for $i \geq n$.

**Proof**

For $i > n$, $H_i(M;\mathbb{Z})=0$ for any $n$-manifold, as $M$ has the homotopy type of an $n$-dimensional CW complex, and the cellular chain groups of an $n$-dimensional complex are trivial for degrees greater than $n$.

For $i=n$, to prove $H_n(M) = 0$, let $[c] \in H_n(M)$. This class corresponds to a section of the orientation sheaf $M_{\mathbb{Z}} \to M$. Since any cycle representing $[c]$ has a compact image, this section has compact support. For a non-compact manifold, any section with compact support must be the zero section, implying $[c]=0$.
# 2.
Show that the Euler characteristic of a closed, oriented, $(4n+2)$-dimensional manifold is even.

**Proof**

By the definition of the Euler characteristic we have

$
\chi(M)=\sum_{i=0}^{4 n+2}(-1)^i \cdot \operatorname{rk}\left(H^i(M)\right) .
$

By Poincaré duality, we obtain

$$
\operatorname{rk}\left(H^i(M)\right)=\operatorname{rk}\left(H^{4n+2-i}(M)\right) .
$$


Therefore,

$
\chi(M) \equiv \operatorname{rk}\left(H^{2 n+1}(M)\right)\pmod 2.
$

Let us now consider the following cup product pairing

$$
H^{2 n+1}(M) \times H^{2 n+1}(M) \xrightarrow{\cup} H^{4 n+2}(M) \xrightarrow{\langle\cdot,[M]\rangle} \mathbb{Z}
$$

defined by

$$
(\alpha, \beta) \mapsto \alpha \cup \beta \mapsto \langle\alpha \cup \beta, [M]\rangle .
$$

By Poincaré Duality, after moding out by torsion, this pairing is nonsingular. As a result, the matrix $A$ of the cup product pairing is non-singular and anti-symmetric. By linear algebra, $A$ is similar to a matrix with diagonal blocks

$`
\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & 1 \\
-1 & 0
\end{array}\right)
`$

Therefore,

$
\operatorname{rk}\left(H^{2 n+1}(M)\right)=\operatorname{rk}(A),
$

which is clearly even.
# 3.
Let $M$ be a closed, oriented $4 n$-dimensional manifold, with fundamental class $[M]$. The middle intersection pairing
$$
(,): H^{2 n}(M ; \mathbb{Z}) / \text{Torsion} \otimes H^{2 n}(M ; \mathbb{Z}) / \text{Torsion} \to \mathbb{Z}
$$
given by $(\alpha, \beta)=\langle\alpha \cup \beta,[M]\rangle$ is symmetric and nondegenerate. Let $`\left\{\alpha_1, \cdots, \alpha_r\right\}`$ be a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $H^{2 n}(M ; \mathbb{Z})/\text{Torsion}$, and let $A=(a_{i j})$ for $a_{i j}:=(\alpha_i, \alpha_j) \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then $A$ is a symmetric matrix with $\det(A)= \pm 1$, so it is diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$. Define the signature of $M$ to be

$\sigma(M) := \text{(number of positive eigenvalues)} - \text{(number of negative eigenvalues)}$

(a) Compute $\sigma\left(\mathbb{C P}^{2 n}\right), \sigma\left(S^2 \times S^2\right)$.

**Proof**

1.  **$\sigma(\mathbb{CP}^{2n})$:** The cohomology ring of $\mathbb{CP}^{2n}$ is generated by a class $x \in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^{2n}; \mathbb{Z})$ with the relation $x^{2n+1}=0$. The middle cohomology group $H^{2n}(\mathbb{CP}^{2n}; \mathbb{Z})$ is generated by $x^n$. The intersection form on this generator is given by $(x^n, x^n) = \langle x^{2n}, [\mathbb{CP}^{2n}] \rangle = 1$. Thus, the matrix of the intersection form is $(1)$, which has one positive eigenvalue and no negative eigenvalues. Therefore, $\sigma(\mathbb{CP}^{2n}) = 1 - 0 = 1$.

2.  **$\sigma(S^2 \times S^2)$:** The cohomology ring of $S^2 \times S^2$ is generated by classes $a, b \in H^2(S^2 \times S^2; \mathbb{Z})$ corresponding to the two factors, with the relation that $a^2 = b^2 = 0$. The middle cohomology group is $H^2(S^2 \times S^2; \mathbb{Z})$, with basis $\{a, b\}$. The intersection form on this group has pairings given by $(a, a) = (b, b) = 0$ and $(a, b) = (b, a) = 1$. Thus, the matrix of the intersection form in the basis $\{a, b\}$ is
   
      $`\begin{pmatrix}
      0 & 1 \\
      1 & 0
      \end{pmatrix}`$
      
      which has one positive eigenvalue and one negative eigenvalue. Therefore, $\sigma(S^2 \times S^2) = 1 - 1 = 0$.

(b) Show that the signature $\sigma(M)$ is congruent $\bmod 2$ to the Euler characteristic $\chi(M)$.

**Proof**

By Poincaré duality, $b_i(M) = b_{4n-i}(M)$, which implies:
$$
\chi(M) = \sum_{i=0}^{4n} (-1)^i b_i(M) = 2 \sum_{i=0}^{2n-1} (-1)^i b_i(M) + b_{2n}(M) \equiv b_{2n}(M) \pmod 2.
$$
The signature $\sigma(M) = p - q$ and $b_{2n}(M) = p + q$, where $p$ and $q$ are the number of positive and negative eigenvalues of the intersection form. Since $p - q \equiv p + q \pmod 2$, we have $\sigma(M) \equiv b_{2n} \equiv \chi(M) \pmod 2$.
# 4.
Let $M$ be a closed, connected, orientable 4-manifold with fundamental group $\pi_1(M) \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ and Euler characteristic $\chi(M)=5$.

(a) Compute $H_i(M, \mathbb{Z})$ for all $i$.

**Proof**

1.  **$H_0(M)$ and $H_4(M)$:** Since $M$ is a closed, connected, orientable 4-manifold, $H_0(M) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $H_4(M) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
2.  **$H_1(M)$:** By the Hurewicz theorem, $`H_1(M) \cong \pi_1(M)_{\text{ab}} = (\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})_{\text{ab}} = \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}`$.
3.  **Betti Numbers:** The Betti numbers are $b_0=1, b_1=0, b_4=1$. From the Euler characteristic $\chi(M) = 5$, we have $5 = b_0 - b_1 + b_2 - b_3 + b_4$. By Poincaré Duality over a field, $b_k = b_{4-k}$, so $b_3=b_1=0$. This gives $5 = 1 - 0 + b_2 - 0 + 1 \implies b_2=3$. So the free part of $H_2(M)$ is $\mathbb{Z}^3$ and $H_3(M)$ is a torsion group.
4.  **Torsion Parts:**
    *   For $H_3(M)$: By Poincaré Duality, $H_3(M) \cong H^1(M)$. By the Universal Coefficient Theorem, $H^1(M) \cong \text{Hom}(H_1(M), \mathbb{Z}) \oplus \text{Ext}(H_0(M), \mathbb{Z})$. Since $H_1(M)$ is pure torsion, $\text{Hom}(H_1(M), \mathbb{Z}) = 0$. Since $H_0(M)$ is free, $\text{Ext}(H_0(M), \mathbb{Z}) = 0$. Thus, $H^1(M) = 0$ and $H_3(M) = 0$.
    *   For $H_2(M)$: By Poincaré Duality and UCT, the torsion subgroup $T(H_{k-1}) \cong T(H_{n-k})$. For $n=4, k=2$, $T(H_1) \cong T(H_2)$. Thus, $T(H_2(M)) \cong T(H_1(M)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. With the free part from step 3, this gives $H_2(M) \cong \mathbb{Z}^3\oplus\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$.

(b) Prove that $M$ is not homotopy equivalent to any CW complex with no 3-cells.

**Proof**

Suppose $M$ is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex $X$ with no 3-cells. In the cellular chain complex of $X$, the group $C_3(X)$ is zero, which implies that the boundary map $d_3: C_3(X) \to C_2(X)$ is the zero map. Consequently, $H_2(X) \cong \ker(d_2) \subseteq C_2(X)$. Since $C_2(X)$ is a free abelian group, its subgroup $\ker(d_2)$ must also be free abelian and thus torsion-free. However, from part (a), we know that $H_2(M)$ contains a non-trivial torsion subgroup $T(H_2(M)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. This contradiction shows that $M$ cannot be homotopy equivalent to a CW complex with no 3-cells.
# 5.
Let $M$ be a closed, connected, oriented $n$-manifold and let $f: S^n \rightarrow M$ be a continuous map of non-zero degree, i.e., the morphism
$$
f_*: H_n\left(S^n ; \mathbb{Z}\right) \rightarrow H_n(M ; \mathbb{Z})
$$
is non-trivial. Show that $M$ and $S^n$ have the same $\mathbb{Q}$-homology.

**Proof**

Since $M$ is a closed, connected, oriented $n$-manifold, we have $H_n(M; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. The map $f: S^n \to M$ induces a homomorphism on homology $`f_*: H_n(S^n; \mathbb{Z}) \to H_n(M; \mathbb{Z})`$. Since $`H_n(S^n; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}`$, the map $`f_*`$ can be identified with multiplication by an integer $d$, which is the degree of the map. By assumption, $d \neq 0$.

Over $\mathbb{Q}$, the induced map $`f^*: H^k(M; \mathbb{Q}) \to H^k(S^n; \mathbb{Q})`$ is injective for all $k$. Indeed, if $\alpha \in H^k(M; \mathbb{Q})$ is non-zero, by Poincaré duality there exists $\beta \in H^{n-k}(M; \mathbb{Q})$ such that $\alpha \cup \beta \neq 0$. Then $`f^*(\alpha \cup \beta) = f^*(\alpha) \cup f^*(\beta)`$ is non-zero in $H^n(S^n; \mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Q}$ because $`f^*`$ is an isomorphism in degree $n$ (as $d \neq 0$ implies $`f^*`$ is multiplication by $d$ on the top-dimensional cohomology). This implies $f^*(\alpha) \neq 0$. Since $H^k(S^n; \mathbb{Q}) = 0$ for $0 < k < n$, we must have $H^k(M; \mathbb{Q}) = 0$ for $0 < k < n$. Thus, $M$ and $S^n$ have the same $\mathbb{Q}$-homology.
# 6.
Show that there is no orientation-reversing continuous map $\mathbb{CP}^{2 n} \rightarrow \mathbb{CP}^{2 n}$.

**Proof**

For any continuous map $f: \mathbb{CP}^{2n} \to \mathbb{CP}^{2n}$, the induced map on the second cohomology group $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^{2n}; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ must be multiplication by some integer $a$:

$$f^*(x) = ax, \quad \text{where } a \in \mathbb{Z}$$

Because $f^*$ is a ring homomorphism, the effect on the top-dimensional cohomology class (the orientation) is determined by that same integer:

$`f^*(x^{2n}) = (f^*(x))^{2n} = (ax)^{2n} = a^{2n}x^{2n}`$

Since $2n$ is an even power, $a^{2n}$ will always be $\ge 0$ for any integer $a$. If $f$ is orientation-reversing, then $f^*(x^{2n}) = -1 \cdot x^{2n}$. This would require $a^{2n} = -1$, which is impossible for any integer $a$.
