# 11.19
Let $R$ be a ring, $S$ a multiplicative subset. Prove $S^{-1}R = 0$ if and only if $S$ contains a nilpotent element.

**Proof**

($\Rightarrow$) Assume $S^{-1}R = 0$. This means that for every element $r \in R$, there exists an element $s \in S$ such that $s \cdot r = 0$. In particular, for the element $1 \in R$, there exists an element $s_0 \in S$ such that $s_0 \cdot 1 = 0$, which implies that $s_0 = 0$. Since $s_0$ is in $S$, we have found a nilpotent element in $S$.

($\Leftarrow$) Now assume that $S$ contains a nilpotent element, say $s_0$. Since $S$ is a multiplicative subset, all powers of $s_0$ are also in $S$. Let $n$ be the smallest positive integer such that $s_0^n = 0$. Then $S^{-1}R = 0$ because for any element $r \in R$, we have $s_0^n \cdot r = 0$. Therefore, $S^{-1}R = 0$.

# 12.26
Let $R$ be a ring, $S$ a multiplicative subset, $P$ a projective
module. Then $S^{-1}P$ is a projective $S^{-1}R$-module.

**Proof**

Since $P$ is a projective $R$-module, there exists an $R$-module $Q$ such that $P \oplus Q \cong F$ for some free $R$-module $F$. The localization functor $S^{-1}$ commutes with direct sums, giving an isomorphism $S^{-1}P \oplus S^{-1}Q \cong S^{-1}(P \oplus Q)$ via the map $(p/s, q/s) \mapsto (p,q)/s$. Since $F$ is free, $F \cong \bigoplus_{i \in I} R$, and because localization commutes with direct sums, $S^{-1}F \cong \bigoplus_{i \in I} S^{-1}R$, which is a free $S^{-1}R$-module. Thus $S^{-1}P$ is a direct summand of a free $S^{-1}R$-module, which implies that $S^{-1}P$ is a projective $S^{-1}R$-module.
# 12.27
Let $R$ be a ring, $S$ a multiplicative subset, $M, N$ modules.
Show $S^{-1}(M \otimes_R N) = S^{-1}M \otimes_R N = S^{-1}M \otimes_{S^{-1}R} S^{-1}N = S^{-1}M \otimes_R S^{-1}N$.

**Proof:**

By $S^{-1}M = S^{-1}R \otimes_R M$ and associativity,

$S^{-1}M \otimes_R N \cong (S^{-1}R \otimes_R M) \otimes_R N \cong S^{-1}R \otimes_R (M \otimes_R N) \cong S^{-1}(M \otimes_R N)$.

The isomorphism $S^{-1}M \otimes_R S^{-1}R \cong S^{-1}M$ is given by $m/s \otimes r/t \mapsto rm/st$ with inverse $m/s \mapsto m/s \otimes 1/1$.

Using $S^{-1}M \otimes_R S^{-1}R \cong S^{-1}M$ again, we have $S^{-1}M \otimes_R S^{-1}N \cong (S^{-1}M \otimes_R S^{-1}R) \otimes_{S^{-1}R} S^{-1}N \cong S^{-1}M \otimes_{S^{-1}R} S^{-1}N$.
# 16.24
Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring,

$`\{F_\lambda\}_{\lambda \in \Lambda}`$

a set of polynomials in variables $X_1, \ldots, X_n$. Show there exists a finite subset $\Lambda_0 \subset \Lambda$ such that the set $V_0$ of zeros in $R^n$ of the $F_\lambda$ for $\lambda \in \Lambda_0$ is equal to the set $V$ of zeros of the $F_\lambda$ for $\lambda \in \Lambda$.

**Proof**

Let $P = R[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$. Since $R$ is Noetherian, by Hilbert's Basis Theorem, $P$ is also Noetherian. Let $I$ be the ideal generated by the set of polynomials $`\{F_\lambda\}_{\lambda \in \Lambda}`$. Since $P$ is a Noetherian ring, the ideal $I$ is finitely generated. This implies that there exists a finite subset $\Lambda_0 \subset \Lambda$ such that the ideal generated by $`\{F_\lambda\}_{\lambda \in \Lambda_0}`$ is $I$ itself. The set of common zeros of a set of polynomials depends only on the ideal they generate. Thus $`V = V(\{F_\lambda\}_{\lambda \in \Lambda}) = V(I)`$ and $`V_0 = V(\{F_\lambda\}_{\lambda \in \Lambda_0}) = V(I)`$. Therefore, $V = V_0$.
# 16.25
Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring, $F := \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n X^n \in R[[X]]$ a power series in one variable. Show that $F$ is nilpotent if and only if each $a_n$ is nilpotent.

**Proof**

($\Rightarrow$) Let $\mathfrak{p} \subset R$ be a prime ideal. The quotient map $R \to R/\mathfrak{p}$ induces a homomorphism $R[[X]] \to (R/\mathfrak{p})[[X]]$. If $F$ is nilpotent, its image $\bar{F}$ in $(R/\mathfrak{p})[[X]]$ is also nilpotent. Since $R/\mathfrak{p}$ is an integral domain, the power series ring $(R/\mathfrak{p})[[X]]$ is also an integral domain. Thus $\bar{F} = 0$, which implies $`\bar{a}_n = 0`$ for all $n$, so $a_n \in \mathfrak{p}$. Since this holds for every prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$, each $a_n$ belongs to the nilradical $\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} R} \mathfrak{p}$, and is therefore nilpotent.

($\Leftarrow$) Conversely, suppose each $a_n$ is nilpotent. In a Noetherian ring, the ideal $I = (a_0, a_1, \dots)$ generated by the coefficients is finitely generated, thus nilpotent, there exists $m$ such that $I^m = 0$, implying $F^m = 0$.
# Problem A.
Let $S \subset \mathbb{R}[x]$ be the set of all polynomials $p$ such that $p(0) \neq 0$ and $p(1) \neq 0$. How many maximal ideals does the localization $(\mathbb{R}[x])\left[S^{-1}\right]$ have?

**Proof**

The maximal ideals of the localization $(\mathbb{R}[x])[S^{-1}]$ correspond to the prime ideals of $\mathbb{R}[x]$ that are maximal among those disjoint from $S$. Since $S$ consists of all polynomials $p$ such that $p(0) \neq 0$ and $p(1) \neq 0$, an ideal $I$ is disjoint from $S$ if and only if every $p \in I$ satisfies $p(0) = 0$ or $p(1) = 0$. If $I$ is not contained in $(x)$ and not contained in $(x-1)$, there exist $f \in I \setminus (x)$ and $g \in I \setminus (x-1)$. Then $f(0) \neq 0$ and $g(1) \neq 0$. Consider $h = f(x-1) + gx$; one can verify $h(0) \neq 0$ and $h(1) \neq 0$, so $h \in S \cap I$, a contradiction. Thus $I \subseteq (x)$ or $I \subseteq (x-1)$. Therefore, the maximal ideals are exactly $(x)$ and $(x-1)$.
# Problem B.
Let $R$ be a ring and let $S, T \subset R$ be two multiplicative sets.

(a) Suppose that, for any $s \in S$, there exists $t \in T$ such that $t \in(s)$. Construct a homomorphism of $R$-algebras $R\left[S^{-1}\right] \rightarrow R\left[T^{-1}\right]$.

(b) Conversely, suppose that a homomorphism of $R$-algebras $R\left[S^{-1}\right] \rightarrow R\left[T^{-1}\right]$ exists. Show that $S$ and $T$ satisfy the above condition.

**Proof**

(a) By the universal property of localization, an $R$-algebra homomorphism $\phi: R[S^{-1}] \to R[T^{-1}]$ exists if and only if the image of every $s \in S$ under the natural map $\eta: R \to R[T^{-1}]$ is a unit. For any $s \in S$, there exists $t \in T$ such that $t \in (s)$, meaning $t = as$ for some $a \in R$. In $R[T^{-1}]$, $t$ is a unit. Since $t = as$, we have $\eta(s) \cdot (a/t) = (s/1) \cdot (a/t) = (as)/t = t/t = 1$, so $\eta(s)$ is a unit. Thus, the universal property provides a unique $R$-algebra homomorphism $\phi$ defined by $\phi(r/s) = ra/t$.

(b) Suppose an $R$-algebra homomorphism $\phi: R[S^{-1}] \to R[T^{-1}]$ exists. For any $s \in S$, $s/1$ is a unit in $R[S^{-1}]$, so its image $\phi(s/1) = s/1$ must be a unit in $R[T^{-1}]$. This means there exists $r/t \in R[T^{-1}]$ such that $(s/1)(r/t) = 1$ in $R[T^{-1}]$. Thus, there exists $u \in T$ such that $u(sr - t) = 0$, which implies $usr = ut$. Since $u, t \in T$ and $T$ is a multiplicative set, $ut \in T$. Let $t' = ut$. Then $t' = (ur)s \in (s)$, so there exists $t' \in T$ such that $t' \in (s)$.
